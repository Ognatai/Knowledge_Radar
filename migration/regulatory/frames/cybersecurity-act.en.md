### TL;DR

The Cybersecurity Act has two parts. It gives ENISA, the European Union Agency for Cybersecurity, a permanent mandate and sets out its tasks, from supporting EU cybersecurity policy and the CSIRTs network to awareness-raising and exercises. And it creates the European cybersecurity certification framework: ENISA prepares certification schemes for ICT products, services and processes, and since 2025 also for managed security services, which the Commission adopts by implementing acts. Certificates are issued at the assurance levels basic, substantial or high, are recognised throughout the EU and are voluntary unless other law makes them mandatory. Other acts such as the [[nis2-directive|NIS2 Directive]] and the [[cyber-resilience-act|Cyber Resilience Act]] build on these schemes.

### Scope

**Subject matter** (Art. 1): objectives, tasks and organisation of ENISA, and a framework for European cybersecurity certification schemes for ICT products, ICT services, ICT processes and managed security services. Member State competences for public security, defence, national security and criminal law remain unaffected (Art. 1(2)).

**Who**: ENISA and its bodies; the Commission, Member States and their national cybersecurity certification authorities; manufacturers and providers who seek certification or issue EU statements of conformity; conformity assessment bodies.

**Certification framework** (Title III): schemes define security objectives (Art. 51, 51a), assurance levels (Art. 52) and their elements (Art. 54). Certification is voluntary unless Union or national law makes it mandatory (Art. 56(2)). For low-risk products, schemes can allow conformity self-assessment with an EU statement of conformity (Art. 53).

**Structure**: four titles: general provisions (Title I), ENISA with its mandate, tasks, organisation, budget and staff (Title II, Art. 3–45), the cybersecurity certification framework (Title III, Art. 46–65) and final provisions (Title IV), plus an annex with requirements for conformity assessment bodies.

### Enforcement and penalties

- **Supervision** (Art. 58): national cybersecurity certification authorities monitor certificates and self-assessments, handle complaints, can audit, withdraw certificates and impose penalties.
- **Penalties** (Art. 65): Member States lay down effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties; the Regulation sets no amounts. In Germany, § 65(4) BSIG provides fines of up to 500,000 euros for breaches of the information duties under Articles 55 and 56(8).
- **Remedies** (Art. 63, 64): natural and legal persons can complain to the issuer of a certificate and seek judicial remedy.
- **Peer review** (Art. 59): national certification authorities are reviewed by at least two other authorities and the Commission at least every five years.

### Relationship to other acts

- **[[nis2-directive|NIS2 Directive]]**: the Cybersecurity Act still refers to the first NIS Directive (EU) 2016/1148; NIS2 repealed it, and references are read as references to NIS2 (Art. 44 NIS2). Member States may require NIS2 entities to use certified products (Art. 24 NIS2).
- **[[cyber-resilience-act|Cyber Resilience Act]]**: certificates under European schemes can give a presumption of conformity with the CRA's essential requirements and may become mandatory for critical products (Art. 8, 27 CRA).
- **[[eu-ai-act|EU AI Act]]**: certificates or statements of conformity under European cybersecurity schemes can give a presumption of conformity with the AI Act's cybersecurity requirements for high-risk AI systems (Article 42(2) AI Act).
- **[[bsig|BSI Act]]**: designates the BSI as Germany's national cybersecurity certification authority (§ 54 BSIG) and allows an ordinance to require certified products for NIS2 entities (§ 30(6) BSIG).
- **[[gdpr|GDPR]]**: ENISA processes personal data under Regulation (EU) 2018/1725, the data protection regulation for EU bodies (Art. 41).

### Relevance for AI development

- **Certification of AI components**: AI software and AI-enabled products are ICT products, AI-based services can be ICT services; once a European scheme covers them, they can be certified at basic, substantial or high assurance level.
- **AI Act link**: a European cybersecurity certificate covering an AI system can support the presumption of conformity with the AI Act's cybersecurity requirements for high-risk AI systems.
- **Security objectives** (Art. 51): schemes aim at protecting data throughout the life cycle, access control, logging, absence of known vulnerabilities and secure by design and by default, which also applies to models and their data pipelines.
- **Managed security services** (Art. 51a): AI-based security operations centres, threat detection or penetration testing services can be certified under future schemes for managed security services.
- **ENISA guidance** (Art. 9): ENISA analyses emerging technologies and their impact on cybersecurity, which in practice includes AI threat landscapes and guidance on securing machine learning.
