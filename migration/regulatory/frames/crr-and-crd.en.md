### TL;DR

The Capital Requirements Regulation (CRR) and the Capital Requirements Directive (CRD) implement the Basel III standards in the EU and form the core of European banking supervision. The CRR applies directly and sets the quantitative rules: what counts as own funds, how much capital banks must hold against credit, market, operational and CVA risk (at least 4.5 % Common Equity Tier 1, 6 % Tier 1 and 8 % total capital, plus a 3 % leverage ratio), limits on large exposures, liquidity ratios, reporting and disclosure. The CRD must be transposed into national law (in Germany mainly through the [[kwg|KWG]]) and governs authorisation, qualifying holdings, governance, remuneration, the supervisory review and evaluation process (SREP), additional own funds requirements, capital buffers and penalties. CRR III and CRD VI (2024) added the output floor, new approaches for credit, market and operational risk, ESG risks, crypto-assets and rules for third-country branches.

### Scope

**Who**: credit institutions and certain large investment firms (institutions), financial holding companies and mixed financial holding companies; the CRD additionally covers branches of third-country credit institutions. Smaller investment firms fall under the separate investment firm regime (Regulation (EU) 2019/2033).

**Division of labour**: the CRR contains the directly applicable prudential requirements (Pillar 1 and Pillar 3); the CRD contains authorisation, supervision and the supervisory review (Pillar 2), capital buffers and penalties, which Member States transpose.

**Structure**: The CRR is divided into parts on general provisions and the level of application, own funds and eligible liabilities, capital requirements for credit, market, operational, settlement and CVA risk, large exposures, liquidity, leverage, reporting, disclosure, delegated and implementing acts, and transitional and final provisions. The CRD is divided into titles on competent authorities (Title II), authorisation, qualifying holdings, mergers and divisions (Title III), freedom of establishment and services (Title V), third countries (Title VI), prudential supervision with the principles of supervision, the review processes, consolidated supervision and capital buffers (Title VII), disclosure by competent authorities (Title VIII) and transitional and final provisions (Title XI). ### Enforcement and penalties

- **Supervisory measures** (CRD Art. 102–105): competent authorities can require additional own funds (Pillar 2 requirement), reinforced arrangements, restrictions of business, limits on variable remuneration and distributions, and specific liquidity requirements; they also communicate guidance on additional own funds.
- **Distribution restrictions** (CRD Art. 141–142): an institution that does not meet the combined buffer requirement may only make distributions up to the maximum distributable amount and must submit a capital conservation plan within five working days.
- **Administrative penalties** (CRD Art. 65–67): for legal persons up to **10 %** of total annual net turnover, for natural persons up to **EUR 5 million**, or up to twice the profits gained or losses avoided; temporary bans on members of the management body and withdrawal of authorisation are possible.
- **Publication** (CRD Art. 68): final administrative penalties are published on the competent authority's website.

### Relationship to other acts

- **[[kwg|KWG]]**: transposes the CRD in Germany and contains the national supplements to the CRR, such as capital buffers and the authorisation requirement.
- **[[marisk|MaRisk]]** and **[[eba-governance-and-outsourcing-guidelines|EBA guidelines on internal governance and outsourcing]]**: specify the governance and risk management requirements of CRD Art. 74 and 88.
- **[[bcbs-239-and-ecb-rdarr-guide|BCBS 239 and ECB RDARR guide]]**: specify the expectations for risk data aggregation and risk reporting that underlie the CRD governance requirements.
- **[[dora|DORA]]**: governs ICT risk management of financial entities; for credit institutions, the competent authorities under the CRD supervise compliance with DORA.
- **[[gdpr|GDPR]]**: personal data is processed under the CRD in accordance with Union data protection law (CRD Art. 62).

### Relevance for AI development

- **Internal models** (CRR Art. 142–191, 325az–325bp): banks that calculate capital requirements with internal ratings-based or market risk models need supervisory permission and must meet requirements for rating systems, the use of models, data maintenance, validation and governance; machine learning in such models is subject to these requirements.
- **Benchmarking and review of models** (CRD Art. 78, 101): competent authorities compare the results of internal approaches across institutions and review the permission at least every three years.
- **Governance and operational risk** (CRD Art. 74, 85): robust governance arrangements, internal controls and policies for operational risk, including risks from outsourcing, also cover AI systems and AI services from third parties.
- **Credit granting** (CRD Art. 79): credit granting must be based on sound, well-defined criteria, and credit risk assessment must not rely solely or mechanically on external ratings.
- **Disclosure** (CRR Part Eight): institutions disclose their risk management objectives and the use of the IRB approach and internal models.
