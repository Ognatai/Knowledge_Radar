### TL;DR

The Digital Markets Act (DMA) sets rules for large digital platforms that act as gatekeepers between businesses and consumers. The Commission designates undertakings as gatekeepers for specific core platform services, such as search engines, app stores, operating systems, social networks, browsers, virtual assistants, cloud and advertising services. Gatekeepers must comply with a list of obligations and prohibitions directly, without a prior finding of abuse: for example, they may not combine personal data across services without consent, prefer their own services in rankings, or prevent third-party app stores. The Commission enforces the DMA with fines of up to 10 % of worldwide turnover, 20 % for repeated infringements.

### Scope

**Who** (Art. 1(2)): core platform services provided or offered by gatekeepers to business users established in the EU or end users established or located in the EU, regardless of where the gatekeeper is established.

**Core platform services** (Art. 2(2)): online intermediation services (e.g. app stores and marketplaces), online search engines, online social networking services, video-sharing platform services, number-independent interpersonal communications services, operating systems, web browsers, virtual assistants, cloud computing services and online advertising services.

**Gatekeepers** (Art. 3): undertakings with a significant impact on the internal market, a core platform service that is an important gateway for business users, and an entrenched and durable position. This is presumed where, among other things, EU turnover is at least EUR 7.5 billion or market capitalisation at least EUR 75 billion, and the service has at least 45 million monthly active end users and 10 000 yearly active business users in the EU.

### Enforcement and penalties

- **Supervision**: the Commission is the sole enforcer of the DMA; it can open market investigations (Art. 16-19), request information, conduct interviews and inspections (Art. 21-23), order interim measures (Art. 24) and make commitments binding (Art. 25).
- **Fines** (Art. 30): up to **10 %** of total worldwide turnover for non-compliance, up to **20 %** for repeated infringements, and up to **1 %** for incorrect, incomplete or misleading information.
- **Periodic penalty payments** (Art. 31): up to **5 %** of average daily worldwide turnover.
- **Systematic non-compliance** (Art. 18): after a market investigation, the Commission can impose behavioural or structural remedies.
- **Private enforcement**: representative actions under Directive (EU) 2020/1828 (Art. 42); national courts apply the DMA in coordination with the Commission (Art. 39).

### Relationship to other acts

- **EU competition law** (Art. 101, 102 TFEU): applies in parallel; the DMA sets ex ante obligations that do not depend on proving an abuse of dominance.
- **[[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]]**: complementary; the DSA regulates content and systemic risks, the DMA market power.
- **[[gdpr|GDPR]]**: the ban on combining personal data without consent refers to consent under the GDPR (Art. 5(2)).
- **[[data-act|Data Act]]**: gatekeepers are not eligible third parties for receiving data under Art. 5 of the Data Act.
- **[[eidas|eIDAS Regulation]]**: gatekeepers must give providers of European Digital Identity Wallets access to operating system and hardware features (Art. 12b eIDAS).

### Relevance for AI development

- **Virtual assistants and cloud** (Art. 2(2)): virtual assistants and cloud computing services can be core platform services; AI assistants of gatekeepers may therefore fall under the obligations once designated.
- **Combining data** (Art. 5(2)): gatekeepers may not combine or cross-use personal data from different services, e.g. to train or personalise models, without the user's consent.
- **Business users' data** (Art. 6(2)): gatekeepers may not use non-public data of business users to compete with them, including for training competing products.
- **Data portability and access** (Art. 6(9), 6(10)): end users and business users can obtain their data continuously and in real time, which can feed third-party analytics and AI services.
- **Search data** (Art. 6(11)): gatekeepers with search engines must give competitors access to ranking, query, click and view data on fair terms, relevant for training search and ranking models.
- **Self-preferencing** (Art. 6(5)): ranking algorithms may not treat the gatekeeper's own services more favourably.
