### TL;DR

The eIDAS Regulation sets EU-wide rules for electronic identification and trust services. It requires Member States to recognise each other's notified electronic identification means and, since its 2024 revision ("eIDAS 2.0"), to provide every citizen and resident with a European Digital Identity Wallet. It also regulates trust services such as electronic signatures, seals, time stamps, registered delivery, website authentication certificates and, newly, electronic attestations of attributes, electronic archiving and electronic ledgers. Qualified trust services are supervised and enjoy specific legal effects across the EU.

### Scope

**Subject matter** (Art. 1): conditions for the mutual recognition of electronic identification means and for European Digital Identity Wallets; rules for trust services; a legal framework for electronic signatures, seals, time stamps, documents, registered delivery services, website authentication, electronic archiving, electronic attestation of attributes, remote signature and seal creation devices and electronic ledgers.

**Who** (Art. 2): electronic identification schemes notified by Member States, European Digital Identity Wallets provided by Member States, and trust service providers established in the EU. Closed systems based on agreements are excluded.

**Qualified and non-qualified**: qualified trust services must meet additional requirements, are audited at least every 24 months (Art. 20) and are listed in national trusted lists (Art. 22); in return they enjoy specific legal effects, such as the equivalence of a qualified electronic signature with a handwritten signature (Art. 25(2)).

### Enforcement and penalties

- **Supervision**: Member States designate supervisory bodies for providers of European Digital Identity Wallets (Art. 46a) and for trust services (Art. 46b); qualified trust service providers are supervised ex ante and ex post, non-qualified ones ex post.
- **Penalties** (Art. 16): Member States lay down penalties; administrative fines for trust service providers have a maximum of at least **EUR 5 million** or, for legal persons, **1 %** of total worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.
- **Liability** (Art. 11, 13): notifying Member States and trust service providers are liable for damage caused intentionally or negligently by failure to comply with their obligations.

### Relationship to other acts

- **[[gdpr|GDPR]]**: applies to personal data processed in wallets and trust services; the technical framework of the wallet must prevent tracking, linking or correlating user behaviour (Art. 5a(16)).
- **NIS2 Directive (EU) 2022/2555**: trust service providers are subject to the cybersecurity risk management and incident reporting obligations of NIS2.
- **[[digital-markets-act|Digital Markets Act]]**: gatekeepers must give wallet providers access to hardware and software features (Art. 12b).
- **[[digital-services-act|Digital Services Act]]**: very large online platforms under the DSA must accept the wallet at the user's request (Art. 5f(3)).

### Relevance for AI development

- **Proof of origin**: qualified electronic signatures, seals and time stamps can prove the origin and integrity of data, e.g. datasets, model files or documentation, in a legally recognised way.
- **Electronic attestation of attributes** (Art. 45b-45h): verified attributes such as qualifications or age can be presented selectively from the wallet; applications can verify attributes without collecting full identity data.
- **Data minimisation in identification**: wallets must support selective disclosure of attributes (Art. 5a(4)), which limits the personal data that services receive and process.
- **Electronic ledgers** (Art. 45k-45l): qualified electronic ledgers enjoy a presumption of the integrity and chronological order of their records, which can support auditable logs.
- **Automated processes**: an electronic signature is by definition created by a natural person (Art. 3(9)) and an electronic seal by a legal person (Art. 3(24)); documents generated automatically, e.g. by AI systems, can therefore be sealed by the organisation responsible for them.
