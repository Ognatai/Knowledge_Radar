### TL;DR

The Data Act sets EU-wide rules on who may access and use data generated by connected products and related services. Users of connected devices, such as cars, machines or smart home devices, get a right to access their data and to share them with third parties. Data holders must make data available on fair terms, unfair contract terms on data are void, public bodies can request data in exceptional situations, and customers must be able to switch between cloud and other data processing services. It covers personal and non-personal data and applies without prejudice to the [[gdpr|GDPR]].

### Scope

**Subject matter** (Art. 1(1)): access to product data and related service data for users of connected products; making data available by data holders to data recipients; access by public sector bodies in cases of exceptional need; switching between data processing services; safeguards against unlawful third-country governmental access to non-personal data; interoperability.

**Data covered** (Art. 1(2)): personal and non-personal data. Where personal data are concerned, the GDPR and the ePrivacy rules remain unaffected and prevail in case of conflict (Art. 1(5)).

**Who**: manufacturers of connected products and providers of related services placed on the EU market, users of such products, data holders and data recipients, public sector bodies, and providers of data processing services such as cloud services.

**Structure**: eleven chapters, including data sharing with consumers and businesses (Chapter II, Art. 3-7), obligations for data holders obliged to make data available (Chapter III), unfair contractual terms (Chapter IV), data for public sector bodies (Chapter V), switching between data processing services (Chapter VI), international governmental access (Chapter VII) and interoperability (Chapter VIII).

### Enforcement and penalties

- **Supervision**: each Member State designates one or more competent authorities and, if there are several, a data coordinator (Art. 37). The data protection authorities are responsible for matters concerning personal data.
- **Penalties** (Art. 40): Member States lay down effective, proportionate and dissuasive penalties. For infringements of Chapters II, III and V, the data protection authorities may, within their competence, impose fines under Art. 83 GDPR.
- **Disputes** (Art. 10): users, data holders and data recipients can use certified dispute settlement bodies.
- **European Data Innovation Board**: the board set up under the [[data-governance-act|Data Governance Act]] supports consistent application.

### Relationship to other acts

- **[[gdpr|GDPR]]**: where data are personal, the GDPR continues to apply and prevails in case of conflict (Art. 1(5)); processing such data still needs a legal basis under the GDPR.
- **[[data-governance-act|Data Governance Act]]**: complementary; the DGA sets up trusted data intermediaries and data altruism, the Data Act creates rights to access and use data.
- **[[digital-markets-act|Digital Markets Act]]**: undertakings designated as gatekeepers are not eligible third parties for receiving data under Art. 5 of the Data Act.
- **Database Directive 96/9/EC**: the sui generis database right does not apply to databases containing data obtained from connected products (Art. 43).
- **[[eu-ai-act|EU AI Act]]**: independent; data accessed under the Data Act may be used for AI training only within the limits of the Data Act, the GDPR and contractual terms.

### Relevance for AI development

- **Access to device data** (Art. 4-5): users of connected products can obtain the data their products generate and have them shared with third parties, which opens up new sources of training data for predictive maintenance, mobility or energy services.
- **Limits on use** (Art. 4, 6): the data may not be used to develop a competing connected product, and third parties may use them only for the purposes agreed with the user.
- **Design obligations** (Art. 3): products placed on the market from 12 September 2026 must make data accessible by default, which affects how data pipelines and device interfaces are designed.
- **Cloud switching** (Chapter VI): customers can switch between data processing services, including those used to train and host models; from 12 January 2027 no switching charges may be imposed (Art. 29).
- **Interoperability** (Art. 33-36): requirements for data spaces and for smart contracts used to execute data sharing agreements.
- **Personal data**: where device data relate to identifiable persons, training on them still needs a legal basis under the GDPR.
