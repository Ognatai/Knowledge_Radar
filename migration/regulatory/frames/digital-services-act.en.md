### TL;DR

The Digital Services Act (DSA) sets EU-wide rules for intermediary services such as internet access providers, hosting services, online platforms and search engines. It keeps the liability exemptions for third-party content, adds due diligence obligations graduated by type and size of service, and gives users rights against content moderation decisions. Very large online platforms and search engines with at least 45 million monthly active users in the EU must also assess and mitigate systemic risks, open their recommender systems and advertising to scrutiny and give researchers access to data. The Commission supervises them directly, with fines of up to 6 % of worldwide annual turnover.

### Scope

**Who** (Art. 2): intermediary services offered to recipients in the EU, regardless of where the provider is established. The obligations build on each other:

- all intermediary services: points of contact, terms and conditions, transparency reports (Art. 11-15);
- hosting services: notice and action, statements of reasons (Art. 16-18);
- online platforms: complaint handling, trusted flaggers, interface design, advertising, recommender systems, protection of minors (Art. 20-28), and for marketplaces traceability of traders (Art. 30-32);
- very large online platforms and search engines: systemic risk management, audits, data access (Art. 33-43).

**Liability** (Art. 4-10): mere conduit, caching and hosting providers are not liable for third-party information under the conditions set out; there is no general monitoring obligation (Art. 8).

**Exceptions**: micro and small enterprises are exempt from the obligations of online platforms unless designated as very large (Art. 19, 29).

### Enforcement and penalties

- **Supervision**: the Member State of the provider's main establishment supervises it through its Digital Services Coordinator; the Commission has exclusive powers for the specific obligations of very large online platforms and search engines (Art. 56).
- **National penalties** (Art. 52): Member States set penalties; maximum fines of **6 %** of annual worldwide turnover, **1 %** for incorrect or incomplete information, periodic penalty payments of up to **5 %** of average daily turnover.
- **Commission fines** (Art. 74, 76): up to **6 %** of total worldwide annual turnover for very large online platforms and search engines, plus periodic penalty payments of up to **5 %** of average daily turnover.
- **Remedies for users**: internal complaint handling (Art. 20), out-of-court dispute settlement (Art. 21), complaints to Digital Services Coordinators (Art. 53) and compensation (Art. 54).

### Relationship to other acts

- **E-Commerce Directive 2000/31/EC**: the DSA replaces its liability rules (Art. 12-15 of the Directive) with Art. 4, 5, 6 and 8 DSA (Art. 89).
- **[[digital-markets-act|Digital Markets Act]]**: complementary; the DMA addresses the market power of gatekeepers, the DSA the handling of content and systemic risks.
- **[[gdpr|GDPR]]**: applies to personal data processed by platforms; the DSA adds, for example, the ban on advertising based on profiling with special categories of data or towards minors (Art. 26, 28).
- **[[eu-ai-act|EU AI Act]]**: AI systems used for content moderation or recommendations remain subject to the DSA; the AI Act adds its own requirements, e.g. transparency for AI-generated content.

### Relevance for AI development

- **Recommender systems** (Art. 27, 38): online platforms must explain the main parameters of their recommender systems; very large platforms must offer at least one option not based on profiling.
- **Automated content moderation** (Art. 14-17, 20): the use of automated tools must be described in the terms and conditions and transparency reports; users receive statements of reasons and can contest decisions, and complaint decisions may not be taken solely by automated means.
- **Systemic risks** (Art. 34-35): very large services must assess risks arising from their algorithmic systems, including recommender, content moderation and advertising systems, and mitigate them, e.g. by testing and adapting algorithms (Art. 35(1)(d)).
- **Advertising** (Art. 26, 39): targeting parameters must be disclosed; very large services keep a public advertisement repository.
- **Data access for research** (Art. 40): vetted researchers can obtain data from very large services to study systemic risks, an important source for research on algorithmic effects.
- **Dark patterns** (Art. 25): interfaces may not deceive or manipulate users, which also affects AI-driven personalisation of interfaces.
