### BCBS 239: Principles for effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting (January 2013)

**I. Overarching governance and infrastructure**

#### Principle 1 — Governance

Requires a bank's risk data aggregation capabilities and risk reporting practices to be subject to strong governance arrangements consistent with other principles and guidance of the Basel Committee.

#### Principle 2 — Data architecture and IT infrastructure

Requires a bank to design, build and maintain data architecture and IT infrastructure that fully supports its risk data aggregation and risk reporting in normal times and in times of stress or crisis.

**II. Risk data aggregation capabilities**

#### Principle 3 — Accuracy and integrity

Requires a bank to generate accurate and reliable risk data, aggregated on a largely automated basis to minimise the probability of errors.

#### Principle 4 — Completeness

Requires a bank to capture and aggregate all material risk data across the banking group, available by business line, legal entity, asset type, industry, region and other groupings.

#### Principle 5 — Timeliness

Requires a bank to generate aggregate and up-to-date risk data in a timely manner, with the precise timing depending on the nature and volatility of the risk and its criticality to the risk profile.

#### Principle 6 — Adaptability

Requires a bank to generate aggregate risk data for a broad range of on-demand and ad hoc reporting requests, including during stress or crisis, for changing internal needs and for supervisory queries.

**III. Risk reporting practices**

#### Principle 7 — Accuracy

Requires risk management reports to convey aggregated risk data accurately and precisely and to be reconciled and validated.

#### Principle 8 — Comprehensiveness

Requires risk management reports to cover all material risk areas, with a depth and scope consistent with the size, complexity and risk profile of the bank and the requirements of the recipients.

#### Principle 9 — Clarity and usefulness

Requires risk management reports to communicate information clearly and concisely, tailored to the needs of the recipients and comprehensive enough for informed decision-making.

#### Principle 10 — Frequency

Requires the board and senior management to set the frequency of risk reports according to the needs of the recipients, the nature of the risk and the speed at which it can change.

#### Principle 11 — Distribution

Requires risk management reports to be distributed to the relevant parties while maintaining confidentiality.

**IV. Supervisory review, tools and cooperation**

#### Principle 12 — Review

Requires supervisors to review and evaluate periodically a bank's compliance with Principles 1 to 11.

#### Principle 13 — Remedial actions and supervisory measures

Requires supervisors to have and use the tools and resources, including Pillar 2, to require effective and timely remedial action to address deficiencies.

#### Principle 14 — Home/host cooperation

Requires supervisors to cooperate with supervisors in other jurisdictions on the supervision and review of the Principles and on any remedial action.

### ECB Guide on effective risk data aggregation and risk reporting (May 2024)

#### Section 3.1 — Responsibilities of the management body

Requires the management body to oversee risk data aggregation and risk reporting as part of its responsibility for strategy, risk strategy and internal governance under Article 88(1) CRD, with responsibilities clearly allocated between its supervisory and management functions.

#### Section 3.2 — Sufficient scope of application

Requires the data governance framework to cover all material legal entities, risks and business lines, financial and supervisory reporting processes and the entire lifecycle of the data.

#### Section 3.3 — Effective data governance framework

Requires a clear allocation of roles, responsibilities and ownership for data quality across business, internal control and IT functions.

#### Section 3.4 — Integrated data architecture

Requires a documented, integrated data architecture at group level, including data taxonomies with a dictionary of business definitions and a metadata repository covering legal entities, risks, reports, critical data elements and models.

#### Section 3.5 — Group-wide data quality management and standards

Requires group-wide policies and processes for effective and complete data quality controls, the remediation of material data quality issues and transparency about remaining limitations.

#### Section 3.6 — Timeliness of internal risk reporting

Requires the frequency and production time of internal risk reports to match how quickly the risk figures can change.

#### Section 3.7 — Effective implementation programmes

Requires institutions that do not yet follow the BCBS 239 best practices to set up implementation programmes with adequate project governance and resources to close the gaps identified in internal or external reviews.

#### Section 4 — Supervisory approach

Describes the guide as a building block of the ECB's 2023–25 work programme on risk data aggregation, with more targeted supervisory activities and a more intrusive use of supervisory powers for severe, long-lasting deficiencies.
