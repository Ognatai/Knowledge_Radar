### AT — General part

#### AT 1 — Purpose of the circular

Sets out, on the basis of section 25a(1) KWG, a flexible and practical framework for institutions' risk management, specifies the requirements for outsourcing (section 25b KWG) and ESG risks (section 26c KWG) and implements the relevant EBA guidelines.

#### AT 2.1 — Addressees

Applies to all institutions under section 1(1b) and section 53(1) KWG that are not significant institutions directly supervised by the ECB, as well as to CRD third-country branches and foreign branches of German institutions.

#### AT 2.2 — Risks

Covers the management of material risks, which management must identify regularly and on an ad hoc basis through a risk inventory (overall risk profile), including risk concentrations.

#### AT 2.3 — Business

Identifies lending, trading and real estate business as particularly risk-relevant types of business and defines what counts as lending business and as a credit decision.

#### AT 3.1 — Overall responsibility of management

Makes all managers, regardless of internal allocation of responsibilities, responsible for a proper business organisation, including developing, promoting and monitoring an appropriate risk culture.

#### AT 3.2 — Responsibility of the supervisory body and its committees

Requires the supervisory body to be appropriately involved and requires management to inform it at least quarterly in text form about the business and risk situation.

#### AT 4.1 — Risk-bearing capacity

Requires the institution to ensure that its material risks, taking risk concentrations into account, are continuously covered by its risk coverage potential.

#### AT 4.2 — Strategies

Requires management to set a sustainable business strategy based on a forward-looking analysis of the business model and a consistent risk strategy.

#### AT 4.3 — Internal control system

Requires rules on organisational structure and processes, risk management and risk control processes, a risk control function and a compliance function.

#### AT 4.3.1 — Organisational structure and processes

Requires incompatible activities to be carried out by different staff members and processes, competences, responsibilities and communication channels to be clearly defined.

#### AT 4.3.2 — Risk management and risk control processes

Requires appropriate processes to identify, assess, manage, monitor and communicate material risks and risk concentrations, usually based on an effective system of limits.

#### AT 4.3.3 — Stress tests

Requires the institution to carry out appropriate stress tests for its material risks regularly and on an ad hoc basis.

#### AT 4.3.4 — Use of models

Sets requirements for models used in risk management processes, expressly including artificial intelligence, such as justified assumptions, data quality, overrides, regular validation and sufficient explainability.

#### AT 4.4.1 — Risk control function

Requires a risk control function that monitors and reports material risks and is separated, up to and including management level, from the units that initiate or conclude business.

#### AT 4.4.2 — Compliance function

Requires a compliance function that counteracts the risks of non-compliance with legal requirements, promotes effective procedures and controls and advises management.

#### AT 4.4.3 — Internal audit

Requires an effective internal audit function with complete and unrestricted rights to information and access, whose planning, methods and quality are reviewed regularly.

#### AT 5 — Organisational guidelines

Requires business to be conducted on the basis of organisational guidelines documented in text form that are suitable as a basis for internal audit.

#### AT 6 — Documentation

Requires business, control and monitoring records to be prepared systematically and comprehensibly for knowledgeable third parties and kept for five years.

#### AT 7.1 — Staff

Requires staffing aligned with business activities and the risk situation, with the necessary knowledge and experience.

#### AT 7.2 — Technical and organisational resources

Requires appropriate technical and organisational resources, sufficient capacity to generate risk data and effective processes to ensure data quality.

#### AT 7.3 — Contingency management

Requires a contingency plan, updated annually and on an ad hoc basis and based on business impact analyses, for activities and processes that constitute critical or important functions.

#### AT 8.1 — New product process

Requires a concept based on an analysis of the risks before business activities in new products or new markets are taken up.

#### AT 8.2 — Changes to operational processes or structures; acquisitions and mergers

Requires an analysis of the effects on control procedures before material changes to the organisation or ICT systems, and a concept before acquisitions or mergers.

#### AT 9 — Outsourcing

Requires a risk analysis for outsourcing that determines which outsourcing arrangements are material and sets requirements for them; ICT services subject to ICT third-party risk management under DORA are outside the scope of AT 9.

### BT — Special part

#### BT 1 — Special requirements for the internal control system

Introduces the special requirements for organisational structure and processes in lending, trading and real estate business (BTO) and for risk management and risk control processes (BTR).

#### BTO 1.1 — Segregation of functions and voting

Requires a clear organisational separation of the front office (Markt) and back office (Marktfolge) in lending business up to and including management level.

#### BTO 1.2 — Requirements for processes in lending business

Requires processes for granting loans, further processing, credit processing control, intensive care, problem loan management and loan loss provisioning, with responsibility outside the front office, including an assessment of debt service capacity and an at least annual assessment of counterparty risk.

#### BTO 1.3 — Requirements for early risk detection and forbearance

Requires procedures for the early identification of increased credit risks using quantitative and qualitative indicators, and rules on concessions to borrowers (forbearance).

#### BTO 1.4 — Risk classification procedures

Requires meaningful risk classification procedures for assessing counterparty risk, whose development, quality and monitoring are the responsibility of units outside the front office.

#### BTO 2 — Trading business

Requires trading to be separated from risk control and from settlement and control up to and including management level, and governs trading, settlement and control and the immediate recording of positions in risk control.

#### BTO 3 — Real estate business

Sets requirements for organisational structure and processes in real estate business, including the separation of front and back office; they do not apply if the book values of all real estate business exceed neither EUR 40 million nor 4 % of total assets.

#### BTR 1 — Counterparty risk

Requires measures to limit counterparty risk and prohibits lending business without a borrower-related limit.

#### BTR 2 — Market price risk

Requires a system of limits for market price risk based on risk-bearing capacity, daily valuation of trading book positions and at least quarterly valuation of the banking book, including interest rate risk.

#### BTR 3 — Liquidity risk

Requires the institution to ensure that it can meet its payment obligations at all times, including managing intraday liquidity risk and sufficiently diversifying funding sources and liquidity buffers.

#### BTR 4 — Operational risk

Requires appropriate management of operational risk with a uniform definition, at least annual identification and assessment of material risks and the recording of loss events.

#### BTR 5 — Credit spread risk in the banking book

Requires the effects of credit spread changes in the banking book to be considered both for the commercial-law result and for market or present values, and to be addressed in risk management.

#### BT 2 — Requirements for risk reporting

Requires regular, comprehensible and meaningful risk reports to management based on complete, accurate and up-to-date data, including an at least quarterly overall risk report by the risk control function.
