### Guidelines on internal governance (EBA/GL/2021/05)

#### Title I — Proportionality

Requires institutions to apply internal governance arrangements in proportion to their size, internal organisation and the nature, scale and complexity of their activities, consistent with their risk profile and business model.

**Title II — Role and composition of the management body and committees**

#### Section 1 — Role and responsibilities of the management body

Gives the management body ultimate and overall responsibility for the institution, including setting and overseeing the strategy, risk strategy and internal governance, with clearly defined management and supervisory duties.

#### Section 2 — Management function of the management body

Requires the management body in its management function to engage actively in the business, take well-informed decisions and implement the strategies, discussing them regularly with the supervisory function.

#### Section 3 — Supervisory function of the management body

Requires the supervisory function to monitor and constructively challenge the strategy and management decisions and to include independent members.

#### Section 4 — Role of the chair of the management body

Requires the chair, as a rule a non-executive member, to lead the management body, ensure an efficient flow of information and promote open and critical discussion.

#### Section 5 — Committees of the management body in its supervisory function

Requires significant institutions to establish risk, nomination and remuneration committees chaired by non-executive members, and sets out the roles of the risk and audit committees and when committees may be combined.

**Title III — Governance framework**

#### Section 6 — Organisational framework and structure

Requires a suitable, transparent and documented organisational structure that the management body fully knows and understands ("know your structure"), and requires institutions to avoid complex and non-transparent structures.

#### Section 7 — Organisational framework in a group context

Requires governance arrangements to be consistent and well integrated across the group, with subsidiaries adopting group-wide policies in line with their own legal obligations.

#### Section 8 — Outsourcing policy

Requires the management body to approve and regularly review an outsourcing policy that considers the impact of outsourcing on the institution's business and risks.

**Title IV — Risk culture and business conduct**

#### Section 9 — Risk culture

Requires an integrated, institution-wide risk culture based on a full understanding of the risks the institution faces, with the tone set from the top by the management body.

#### Section 10 — Corporate values and code of conduct

Requires the management body to adopt and promote high ethical and professional standards through a code of conduct and requires that staff are not discriminated against.

#### Section 11 — Conflict of interest policy at institutional level

Requires policies to identify, assess, manage and mitigate or prevent conflicts of interest at institutional level, for instance between business lines or entities in the group.

#### Section 12 — Conflict of interest policy for staff

Requires policies on conflicts between the institution's interests and the private interests of staff and members of the management body, including a framework for loans and other transactions with members of the management body and their related parties.

#### Section 13 — Internal alert procedures

Requires internal procedures through which staff can report potential or actual breaches through a specific, independent and autonomous channel.

#### Section 14 — Reporting of breaches to competent authorities

Requires competent authorities to provide effective and reliable mechanisms through which institutions' staff can report breaches to them.

**Title V — Internal control framework and mechanisms**

#### Section 15 — Internal control framework

Requires a robust and comprehensive internal control framework in which business lines manage the risks they incur and independent internal control functions provide oversight.

#### Section 16 — Implementing an internal control framework

Makes the management body responsible for establishing and monitoring the adequacy and effectiveness of the internal control framework, processes and mechanisms.

#### Section 17 — Risk management framework

Requires a holistic, institution-wide risk management framework that covers all risk exposures and enables fully informed decisions on risk-taking.

#### Section 18 — New products and significant changes

Requires a documented new product approval policy, approved by the management body, for new markets, products and services and for significant changes, including new outsourcing arrangements and IT changes.

#### Section 19 — Internal control functions

Requires risk management, compliance and internal audit functions that are independent, adequately resourced and led by heads with access to the supervisory function; internal audit must not be combined with another control function.

#### Section 20 — Risk management function

Requires an institution-wide risk management function that is involved early in the risk strategy and in material changes, ensures that all risks are identified, measured and reported, and is led by a head able to challenge decisions.

#### Section 21 — Compliance function

Requires a permanent and effective compliance function, led by a compliance officer, to manage compliance risk across the institution.

#### Section 22 — Internal audit function

Requires an independent and effective internal audit function with sufficient authority, stature and resources that reviews the institution on a risk-based approach.

#### Title VI — Business continuity management

Requires sound business continuity management and recovery plans so that the institution can continue to operate and limit losses in the event of severe business disruption, covering critical resources such as IT systems and cloud services.

#### Title VII — Transparency

Requires strategies, policies and procedures to be communicated clearly and consistently to all relevant staff.

### Guidelines on outsourcing arrangements (EBA/GL/2019/02)

**Title I — Proportionality: group application and institutional protection schemes**

#### Section 1 — Proportionality

Requires institutions, payment institutions and competent authorities to apply the guidelines in proportion to the risk profile, the nature and business model and the scale and complexity of activities.

#### Section 2 — Outsourcing by groups and institutions that are members of an institutional protection scheme

Applies the guidelines on a sub-consolidated and consolidated basis and allows group entities and members of institutional protection schemes to rely on centrally established arrangements under certain conditions.

**Title II — Assessment of outsourcing arrangements**

#### Section 3 — Outsourcing

Requires institutions to establish whether an arrangement with a third party is outsourcing, in particular whether the function is performed on a recurrent or ongoing basis and would normally be performed by the institution itself.

#### Section 4 — Critical or important functions

Requires a function to be treated as critical or important where a defect or failure would materially impair compliance with authorisation conditions, financial performance or the soundness or continuity of services, and sets criteria for the assessment.

**Title III — Governance framework**

#### Section 5 — Sound governance arrangements and third-party risk

Requires the institution-wide risk management framework to cover the risks from all arrangements with third parties, not only outsourcing.

#### Section 6 — Sound governance arrangements and outsourcing

Provides that outsourcing cannot result in the delegation of the management body's responsibilities and that institutions remain fully responsible for complying with all regulatory obligations.

#### Section 7 — Outsourcing policy

Requires the management body to approve, regularly review and implement a written outsourcing policy covering the main phases of the life cycle of outsourcing arrangements.

#### Section 8 — Conflicts of interests

Requires conflicts of interest arising from outsourcing arrangements, including within groups, to be identified, assessed and managed.

#### Section 9 — Business continuity plans

Requires appropriate business continuity plans for outsourced critical or important functions to be maintained and periodically tested.

#### Section 10 — Internal audit function

Requires internal audit to review outsourced activities on a risk-based approach, in particular the outsourcing of critical or important functions.

#### Section 11 — Documentation requirements

Requires an up-to-date register of information on all outsourcing arrangements and timely information of competent authorities about material changes and severe events.

**Title IV — Outsourcing process**

#### Section 12 — Pre-outsourcing analysis

Requires institutions, before outsourcing, to assess whether the function is critical or important, check the supervisory conditions for outsourcing, assess all relevant risks and carry out due diligence on the service provider.

#### Section 13 — Contractual phase

Requires a written agreement with minimum content for outsourcing critical or important functions, including rules on sub-outsourcing, data and system security, access, information and audit rights and termination rights.

#### Section 14 — Oversight of outsourced functions

Requires ongoing, risk-based monitoring of the performance of service providers, focusing on critical or important functions and on the availability, integrity and security of data.

#### Section 15 — Exit strategies

Requires a documented exit strategy for outsourced critical or important functions that is in line with the outsourcing policy and business continuity plans.

#### Title V — Guidelines on outsourcing addressed to competent authorities

Requires competent authorities to assess whether outsourcing changes the conditions of authorisation, to be able to supervise outsourcing effectively and, where necessary, to restrict outsourced functions or require an exit.
