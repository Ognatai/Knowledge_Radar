### TL;DR

Google was founded by Larry Page and Sergey Brin, who met at Stanford in 1995, and was incorporated in August 1998 with the mission "to organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful" (Google, n.d.-a). Today it is the largest business of Alphabet Inc., which reports Google Services (search, YouTube, Android, Chrome, Gemini and more), Google Cloud and "Other Bets" such as Waymo and Isomorphic Labs; Alphabet calls itself an "AI-first company" since 2016 and had 190,820 employees at the end of 2025 (Alphabet, 2026). Many methods and tools in this knowledge base come from Google researchers, from [[mapreduce|MapReduce]] and [[word2vec|Word2Vec]] to the [[vision-transformer|Vision Transformer]], [[kubernetes|Kubernetes]] and the [[ml-test-score|ML Test Score]]. Under the EU [[digital-markets-act|Digital Markets Act]], Alphabet is a designated [[gatekeeper|gatekeeper]] for eight core platform services (European Commission, 2026).

### Mandate and tasks

The founders' mission is "to organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful" (Google, n.d.-a). Google Services covers ads, Android, Chrome, devices, Gmail, Google Drive, Google Gemini, Google Maps, Google Photos, Google Play, Search and YouTube and earns most of its revenue from advertising, plus subscriptions, platforms and devices; Google Cloud offers Google Cloud Platform and Google Workspace, paid mainly through consumption-based fees and subscriptions (Alphabet, 2026). Other Bets include Waymo for autonomous ride-hailing and Isomorphic Labs for AI-driven drug discovery (Alphabet, 2026).

### Structure and working methods

Alphabet reports Google in two segments and puts its non-Google businesses into Other Bets (Alphabet, 2026). Google presents its AI models and research through Google DeepMind and Google Research (Google, n.d.-c). Google publishes AI Principles organised in three parts: bold innovation, responsible development and deployment across the full lifecycle, including human oversight, safety research, bias testing and privacy protection, and collaborative progress with researchers, governments and civil society; it reports on them in annual [[responsible-ai|Responsible AI]] progress reports, most recently in February 2026 (Google, n.d.-b).

### Key outputs

- Large-scale data processing: MapReduce, introduced in 2004 for jobs such as building the web search index ([[mapreduce|MapReduce]]); Kubernetes, which grew out of a decade of container management at Google ([[kubernetes|Kubernetes]]).
- Language and vision models: Word2Vec (2013) ([[word2vec|Word2Vec]]), wordpiece tokenisation in Google's [[machine-translation|neural machine translation]] system ([[tokenisation|tokenisation]]), the Vision Transformer from Google Research (2021) ([[vision-transformer|Vision Transformer]]), and the Gemini model family used in all of its half-billion-user products (Alphabet, 2026).
- Engineering practice: the ML Test Score rubric ([[ml-test-score|ML Test Score]]) and the Site Reliability Engineering approach to monitoring ([[monitoring-and-alerting|monitoring and alerting]]).
- Platforms used in this knowledge base: Dialogflow CX ([[dialogflow-cx|Dialogflow CX]]), Google Analytics 4 ([[web-analytics|web analytics]]) and Google's cloud AI platform ([[cloud-ai-platforms|cloud AI platforms]]).

### Relevance for AI development

Google provides frontier models (Gemini), AI accelerators (TPUs) and a cloud platform for building and deploying AI applications, and it says all fifteen of its products with more than half a billion users use Gemini models (Alphabet, 2026; [[cloud-ai-platforms|cloud AI platforms]]). Much of today's ML engineering vocabulary, from distributed batch processing to production ML testing, comes from its published work. As a DMA gatekeeper, Alphabet may not combine personal data across its designated services without consent, for example for AI features, and must offer choice screens and data portability ([[gatekeeper|gatekeeper]]); as a provider of general-purpose AI models in the EU, it falls under the [[eu-ai-act|AI Act]] and the supervision of the [[ai-office|AI Office]].

### Auftrag und Aufgaben

Die Mission der Gründer lautet „to organize the world's information and make it universally accessible and useful“ (Google, n.d.-a). Google Services umfasst Werbung, Android, Chrome, Geräte, Gmail, Google Drive, Google Gemini, Google Maps, Google Photos, Google Play, die Suche und YouTube und erzielt den Großteil der Umsätze mit Werbung, dazu mit Abonnements, Plattformen und Geräten; Google Cloud bietet die Google Cloud Platform und Google Workspace an, abgerechnet vor allem verbrauchsabhängig und über Abonnements (Alphabet, 2026). Zu den Other Bets gehören Waymo für autonome Fahrdienste und Isomorphic Labs für KI-gestützte Wirkstoffforschung (Alphabet, 2026).

### Aufbau und Arbeitsweise

Alphabet weist Google in zwei Segmenten aus und fasst die übrigen Geschäfte unter Other Bets zusammen (Alphabet, 2026). Seine KI-Modelle und -Forschung präsentiert Google über Google DeepMind und Google Research (Google, n.d.-c). Google veröffentlicht KI-Grundsätze in drei Teilen: mutige Innovation, verantwortungsvolle Entwicklung und Bereitstellung über den gesamten Lebenszyklus, einschließlich menschlicher Aufsicht, Sicherheitsforschung, Tests auf Bias und Schutz der Privatsphäre, sowie gemeinsamer Fortschritt mit Forschung, Staat und Zivilgesellschaft; darüber berichtet es in jährlichen Fortschrittsberichten zu verantwortungsvoller KI, zuletzt im Februar 2026 (Google, n.d.-b).

- Sprach- und Bildmodelle: Word2Vec (2013) ([[word2vec|Word2Vec]]), Wordpiece-Tokenisierung im neuronalen Übersetzungssystem von Google ([[tokenisation|Tokenisierung]]), der Vision Transformer von Google Research (2021) ([[vision-transformer|Vision Transformer]]) und die Gemini-Modellfamilie, die in allen Produkten mit mehr als einer halben Milliarde Nutzenden eingesetzt wird (Alphabet, 2026).

### Bedeutung für die KI-Entwicklung

Google stellt Spitzenmodelle (Gemini), KI-Beschleuniger (TPUs) und eine Cloud-Plattform für Entwicklung und Betrieb von KI-Anwendungen bereit und gibt an, dass alle fünfzehn seiner Produkte mit mehr als einer halben Milliarde Nutzenden Gemini-Modelle verwenden (Alphabet, 2026; [[cloud-ai-platforms|Cloud-KI-Plattformen]]). Ein großer Teil des heutigen Vokabulars im ML Engineering, von verteilter Stapelverarbeitung bis zum Testen produktiver ML-Systeme, stammt aus seinen Veröffentlichungen.
