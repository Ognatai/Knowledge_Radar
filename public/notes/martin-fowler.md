### TL;DR

Martin Fowler is a software author and Chief Scientist at the software delivery company Thoughtworks, known for his books on refactoring, enterprise application architecture and UML, and for his website martinfowler.com, whose articles and "bliki" entries are widely cited definitions of software engineering practices (Fowler, n.d.-a). He co-signed the Manifesto for Agile Software Development in 2001 (Beck et al., 2001). In this knowledge base, his writing is a primary source for [[ci-cd|continuous integration]], [[technical-debt|technical debt]], [[software-testing|testing]], [[infrastructure-as-code|infrastructure as code]] and [[requirements-engineering|requirements]] practices such as user stories. Since 2023, his site has also published a series of memos by Thoughtworks colleagues on using generative AI in software delivery (Böckeler et al., 2023–2026).

### Career

Fowler worked as an independent consultant from 1991 and then joined Thoughtworks, where he holds the title Chief Scientist, which he himself calls "exceedingly inappropriate"; the about page gives both spring 1999 and 2000 as the start of his work there (Fowler, n.d.-a). Since 2017 he has served on Thoughtworks' global leadership team. In the early 2000s he edited the design column of *IEEE Software* for five years, and he retired from conference speaking in 2021 (Fowler, n.d.-a).

### Key contributions

#### Refactoring

Fowler defines refactoring as "a controlled technique for improving the design of an existing code base"; his book *Refactoring* appeared in 1999 and in a second edition in 2018, co-written with Kent Beck (Fowler, n.d.-c; Fowler, n.d.-b). The term "code smell", "a surface indication that usually corresponds to a deeper problem in the system", was coined by Kent Beck while helping Fowler with the book (Fowler, 2006; [[technical-debt|technical debt]]).

#### Enterprise application architecture and patterns

His main interest is designing software systems so that development teams deliver more value, studying patterns of good software architecture and the processes that support design (Fowler, n.d.-a). *Analysis Patterns* (1996) and *Patterns of Enterprise Application Architecture* (2002) collect such patterns; *Domain Specific Languages* (2010, with Rebecca Parsons) and *NoSQL Distilled* (2012, with Pramod J. Sadalage) extend them to language design and data storage (Fowler, n.d.-b).

#### Agile practices and continuous integration

Fowler signed the Agile Manifesto, which values "individuals and interactions over processes and tools", "working software over comprehensive documentation", "customer collaboration over contract negotiation" and "responding to change over following a plan" (Beck et al., 2001). His article on continuous integration defines CI as every team member merging into a shared mainline at least daily, with each merge verified by an automated build including tests, and distinguishes it from continuous delivery and deployment (Fowler, 2024; [[ci-cd|CI/CD]]).

#### Technical debt

His "technical debt quadrant" classifies debt as deliberate or inadvertent and as reckless or prudent (Fowler, 2009; [[technical-debt|technical debt]]).

### Influence and reception

martinfowler.com also publishes articles by other authors after vetting and developmental editing, which makes it a reference site for practices such as the test pyramid, test doubles and infrastructure as code that other notes here cite (Fowler, n.d.-a; [[software-testing|software testing]], [[infrastructure-as-code|infrastructure as code]]).

### Relevance for AI development

The "Exploring Generative AI" series on his site, written mostly by Birgitta Böckeler since July 2023, documents how coding assistants and agents change software delivery, with topics such as context engineering for coding agents, spec-driven development, supply-chain risks and refactoring with AI (Böckeler et al., 2023–2026; [[context-engineering|context engineering]]). Practices Fowler described, such as small refactoring steps, fast automated tests and continuous integration, are the safety net that makes AI-generated code changes reviewable ([[ci-cd|CI/CD]]).

### Werdegang

Fowler arbeitete ab 1991 als unabhängiger Berater und kam dann zu Thoughtworks, wo er den Titel Chief Scientist trägt, den er selbst „exceedingly inappropriate“ nennt; die Über-mich-Seite nennt sowohl Frühjahr 1999 als auch 2000 als Beginn seiner Tätigkeit dort (Fowler, n.d.-a). Seit 2017 gehört er dem globalen Leitungsteam von Thoughtworks an. Anfang der 2000er-Jahre betreute er fünf Jahre lang die Designkolumne von *IEEE Software*, und 2021 zog er sich von Konferenzvorträgen zurück (Fowler, n.d.-a).

### Wichtige Beiträge

#### Refactoring

Fowler definiert Refactoring als „a controlled technique for improving the design of an existing code base“; sein Buch *Refactoring* erschien 1999 und 2018 in zweiter Auflage, gemeinsam mit Kent Beck (Fowler, n.d.-c; Fowler, n.d.-b). Den Begriff „Code Smell“, „a surface indication that usually corresponds to a deeper problem in the system“, prägte Kent Beck, als er Fowler bei dem Buch half (Fowler, 2006; [[technical-debt|technische Schulden]]).

#### Architektur und Muster für Unternehmensanwendungen

Sein Hauptinteresse gilt dem Entwurf von Softwaresystemen, mit denen Entwicklungsteams mehr Wert liefern; dazu untersucht er Muster guter Softwarearchitektur und die Prozesse, die den Entwurf unterstützen (Fowler, n.d.-a). *Analysis Patterns* (1996) und *Patterns of Enterprise Application Architecture* (2002) sammeln solche Muster; *Domain Specific Languages* (2010, mit Rebecca Parsons) und *NoSQL Distilled* (2012, mit Pramod J. Sadalage) übertragen sie auf Sprachentwurf und Datenhaltung (Fowler, n.d.-b).

#### Agile Praktiken und Continuous Integration

Fowler unterzeichnete das Agile Manifest, das „Individuals and interactions over processes and tools“, „Working software over comprehensive documentation“, „Customer collaboration over contract negotiation“ und „Responding to change over following a plan“ höher bewertet (Beck et al., 2001). Sein Artikel zu Continuous Integration definiert CI so, dass alle Teammitglieder mindestens täglich in eine gemeinsame Hauptlinie integrieren und jede Integration durch einen automatisierten Build mit Tests geprüft wird, und grenzt sie von Continuous Delivery und Continuous Deployment ab (Fowler, 2024; [[ci-cd|CI/CD]]).

#### Technische Schulden

Sein „Technical Debt Quadrant“ ordnet Schulden danach, ob sie bewusst oder unbewusst und ob sie leichtsinnig oder umsichtig aufgenommen wurden (Fowler, 2009; [[technical-debt|technische Schulden]]).

### Wirkung und Rezeption

martinfowler.com veröffentlicht auch Artikel anderer Autorinnen und Autoren nach Prüfung und redaktioneller Begleitung, was die Website zu einer Referenz für Praktiken wie die Testpyramide, Test Doubles und Infrastructure as Code macht, die andere Notizen hier zitieren (Fowler, n.d.-a; [[software-testing|Softwaretests]], [[infrastructure-as-code|Infrastructure as Code]]).

### Bedeutung für die KI-Entwicklung

Die Reihe „Exploring Generative AI“ auf seiner Website, seit Juli 2023 überwiegend von Birgitta Böckeler geschrieben, dokumentiert, wie Coding-Assistenten und Agenten die Softwareentwicklung verändern, mit Themen wie Context Engineering für Coding-Agenten, spezifikationsgetriebener Entwicklung, Lieferkettenrisiken und Refactoring mit KI (Böckeler et al., 2023–2026; [[context-engineering|Context Engineering]]). Von Fowler beschriebene Praktiken wie kleine Refactoring-Schritte, schnelle automatisierte Tests und Continuous Integration bilden das Sicherheitsnetz, das KI-generierte Codeänderungen überprüfbar macht ([[ci-cd|CI/CD]]).
